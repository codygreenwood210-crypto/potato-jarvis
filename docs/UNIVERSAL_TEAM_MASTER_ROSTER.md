## Why v2 exists

The 182-role bench had strong coverage but too many independently routable seats. That created avoidable overlap, duplicated handoffs and uncertain ownership. V2 keeps all capabilities but merges overlapping roles into a smaller number of accountable primary operators.

## Operating model

**User → Atlas → Single Responsible Owner → Mission Pod → QA/Trust checks as needed → Judge → Archivist**

- **Atlas** owns mission intake, Mission Contract, priority and selection of the Single Responsible Owner (SRO).
- The **SRO** owns the outcome and integrates specialist input. One deliverable has one accountable owner.
- A normal mission pod is **3–8 primary operators**. Use more only when the work genuinely spans more disciplines; default maximum is 12.
- Embedded specialties are activated inside their primary owner instead of becoming separate handoffs.
- **Judge is independent** and is never the implementation owner for work it certifies.
- **Archivist** joins for retrieval/continuity and closeout, not as a default production participant.
- **Scout** is activated when current/external facts materially affect correctness.
- **Mentor** and **Redline** are exception roles: training/capability repair and high-risk challenge, not default seats.
- Department heads route within their group but do not override the mission SRO.

## Ownership boundaries

- **Judge vs QA-11:** QA-11 tests and reports defects; Judge independently accepts or rejects the mission. Judge never performs the implementation it certifies.
- **Scout vs Radar:** Scout establishes current external truth across any domain; Radar researches markets, customers, competitors and demand for marketing/commercial decisions.
- **Metric vs Lab:** Metric owns product/business instrumentation and KPI truth; Lab owns marketing experiments and CRO, consuming Metric-quality data.
- **Vision vs Signal (Marketing):** Vision owns the visual system and production quality; Signal owns channel-specific content strategy and creative direction for distribution.
- **Launch vs Beacon:** Beacon owns marketing/GTM strategy; Launch owns operational release, publishing, rollout and release coordination.
- **Guard vs Trust:** Guard owns security/privacy risk across products and systems; Trust owns marketing claims, disclosure, spam/platform and brand-safety compliance.
- **Venture vs Forge:** Venture decides what business/product opportunity is worth pursuing; Forge owns getting the selected product built and delivered.
- **Deal vs Bridge:** Deal owns commercial negotiation and business terms; Bridge owns relationship creation, PR, creators, affiliates and partnership pipeline.
- **Yield vs Margin:** Yield designs monetization mechanics and offers; Margin checks whether those mechanics produce acceptable economics and profit.
- **Archivist vs Canon:** Archivist owns universal/project continuity and evidence; Canon owns story-world continuity and the Story Bible only.
- **Atlas vs department heads:** Atlas sets mission ownership and cross-team routing; department heads decide how their own specialists execute inside the assigned scope.

## Routing rules

1. **One mission, one SRO.** Atlas names the accountable owner before substantive work.
2. **Advice does not equal ownership.** Supporting roles recommend; the SRO integrates and decides within the Mission Contract.
3. **Specialty modes do not create handoffs.** Example: DevOps can activate Cloud/SRE/Build/Linux/Network expertise internally without five extra team seats.
4. **Cross-functional gates are conditional.** Guard/Privacy for material security/privacy risk; Trust for commercial claims; QA-11 for substantive verification; Device when runtime/device behavior matters.
5. **Judge is the final independent gate.** PASS requires all three current Judge gates: final proofread integrity, whole-certified-team completion confirmation, and a rubric score of at least 10/10. Scores 1–9 mean REWORK_REQUIRED.
6. **Failure routing is explicit.** Repeated or systemic failure activates Mentor + Redline and can reopen the roster design.
7. **Canonical sources win.** Project repositories, tests, runtime evidence and canon records outrank summaries.
8. **Commercial work keeps Marketing intact.** Beacon still leads the standing Universal Marketing function even though it sits inside the Commercial & Growth Group.
9. **Creative and story canon stay distinct.** Vision owns visual execution; Quill/Canon own narrative structure and story continuity.
10. **Alternate engine/platform specialties remain available.** Unity and Unreal are preserved as embedded engine modes under the Game/Godot routing cluster; Web, Cloud, SRE, Build, Linux, Network, API, IoT and other merged specialties remain explicitly mapped above.

### Mandate
Proof rereads the complete user-facing draft immediately before delivery and verifies that it is correct, evidence-aligned, internally consistent, copy/paste-safe, and exactly expresses the intended answer to the user's request.

Proof checks:
- exact user intent and requirement coverage;
- factual/technical correctness and claim-to-evidence alignment;
- contradictions, stale assumptions and unsupported completion claims;
- literal fidelity of commands, paths, filenames, branches, IDs, links and code;
- accidental Markdown/backslash escaping or formatting corruption;
- whether claimed files, tools, tests, commits, APIs and artifacts actually exist in evidence;
- temporal provenance, especially old-vs-current screenshots/output;
- placeholders, invented endpoints, simulated tool output and circular instructions;
- clarity and whether the user is given the intended next action.

Proof has **STOP-SEND authority**. Any material defect sends the draft back for correction. Any post-approval edit requires re-review. A lightweight Proof pass applies to every response; technical/procedural/high-stakes/tool/file/code/evidence/multi-step responses use the full checklist.
